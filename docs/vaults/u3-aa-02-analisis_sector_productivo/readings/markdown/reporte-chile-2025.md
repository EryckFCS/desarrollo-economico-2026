**GENERAL INFORMATION**

The Geochemical Map of Chile is a government program that since 2011 has been conducted by the Unit of Geochemistry of SERNAGEOMIN (Geological and Mining Survey of Chile). The objective of this program is to promote the sustainable growth of Chile by the definition of geochemical baselines and the identification of mineral resources.

The Unit of Geochemistry is composed by 4 geologists (Fig.1), with a publication rate of 2 products per year, including 1:250,000 scale geochemical atlases, maps and databases, along with technical reports associated with geochemical studies of fluvial basins (Fig. 2).

# Rare Earth Elements (REE) project:

The year 2025 is marked by the generation of the first national-scale geochemical studies focused primarily on rare earth elements (REE). In addition to the publication of the regional study by Lacassie et al. (2025), background information was generated for the design of a multidisciplinary initiative aimed at the exploration of REE in Chile (Fig. 2).

## Página 5

**Geochemical study of residual soils of Chile - Application of IUGS Manual of Standard Methods** **for Establishing the Global Geochemical Reference Network:**

During 2025, the first study of residual soils in Chile was carried out using the methodology of the defined by CGGB in the **IUGS Manual of Standard Methods for Establishing the Global** **Geochemical Reference Network** ( https://www.globalgeochemicalbaselines.eu/datafiles/file/IUGS- CGGB_2022_Manual_of_Standard_Methods_for_Establishing_the_GGRN.pdf ). The preliminary results of this study, carried out in conjunction with the Austral University of Chile (UACH), were published at the XV National Congress of Soil Sciences of Chile (Fig. 3).

**Figure 3.** Screenshot of the poster presented at the XV National Congress of Soil Science in Chile (La Serena, October 2025). In addition to the poster, the paper titled "Residual Soils of the Los Ríos Region: Variability of the Degree of Geochemical Alteration" was also presented at the lecture session. This constitutes the first study of SERNAGEOMIN in the field of residual soil geochemistry in Chile.

## Página 6

**Chile-Honduras cooperation project:**

Given the positive results of the initial phase (2023-2024), the governments of Chile and Honduras decided to extend the cooperation project between their geological and mining services, with a leading role for geochemistry. In December 2025, the first mission was carried out in Honduras, which included training in geochemical sediment sampling for professionals from INHGEOMIN (Honduras) and geology students from the National Autonomous University of Honduras (UNAH). This training focused on the environmental assessment of drainage systems impacted by mining tailings (Fig.4).

**Figure 4.** Training activities carried out in Honduras during Mission 1 of the Chile-Honduras cooperation project. (a) Isla del Tigre, Gulf of Fonseca: recognition of pyroclastic flows. **Chile-El Salvador cooperation project:**

In March 2025, was carried the first mission of the Chile-Republic of El Salvador cooperation project. During this mission, Rafael Mardones (Unit of Geochemistry) and Paulina Vásquez (Unit of Regional Geology) trained professionals from El Salvador on all relevant aspects of geochemical sampling and data collection using smartphone applications (Fig. 5).

# Cooperation agreement in geochemistry, between SERNAGEOMIN (Chile) and Münster University (Germany):

In 2025 a cooperation agreement was signed between the two institutions. This cooperation would allow the mineralogical analysis of drainage sediments collected in Chile in the context of the Geochemical Map of Chile program of SERNAGEOMIN. These analyses will be carried out at the University of Münster, starting in 2026. It is estimated that this will generate relevant information for understanding sedimentary processes in the Andean Range.

# Geochemical map of Latin America (GEGOQ-ASGMI):

Within the framework of the cooperation coordinated by the GEGEOQ-ASGMI geochemistry expert group, the first geochemical maps of Chile, which can be integrated into the Geochemical Map of Latin America, were generated in 2025. These maps were created using the established methodology, designed to generate a geochemical map on a continental scale. The first development of this type of map in Chile was carried out in the context of the university practice of Mr. Jorge Mansilla (Austral University of Chile), who prepared a report that included a guide for the generation of this type of cartography using the ArcGis software (Fig. 7).

# Geochemical sampling of Chile:

During 2025, five geochemical sampling campaigns were carried out. Four of them focused on the northern zone including the Visviri, Collacagüa and Pisiga Sheets in the highlands, the Tocopilla and Quillagüa Sheets in the Coastal Range and one on the southern zone of the country (Rio Bueno Basin). The geological and geographical diversity of the sampled areas demonstrates the usefulness of a standardized methodology for geochemical sampling (Fig. 9).

**Figure 9.** Geographical conditions of the sampled areas during the year 2025. (a, c) Geochemical sampling in the Río Bueno basin, southern Chile. Access to many of the points requires the use of kayaking. (b, d, e) Geochemical sampling in the Altiplano area (Pisiga, Collacagüa and Visviri sheets). Average altitudes exceed 4,000 m above sea level. The presence of "bofedales" (b) is noteworthy. The "llareta" compact trees (d) are characteristic of altitudes between 3,200 and 4,800 m above sea level. Photographs by Pablo Oliva and Juan Pablo Lacassie.
